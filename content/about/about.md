O Foundation (OFDN) works at the intersections of openness, digital rights, access to knowledge and media freedom. We study community media and co-create openly licensed media. We build open, community-led technology. We look at media and technology through the power structures of race, caste, gender and disability. The team is on the [People](/people/) page.

## Partners

**[Whose Knowledge?](https://whoseknowledge.org/)** is a global, multilingual campaign to correct skewed representations of knowledge on the internet. Anasuya Sengupta and Siko Bouterse founded it. Anasuya Sengupta has advised OFDN since the beginning.

**[Wikitongues](https://wikitongues.org)** is a non-profit platform for every language in the world. With contributors in many countries, it records oral histories from more than 7,000 language communities and builds tools for language activists, such as [Poly](https://poly.wikitongues.org), open-source software for word lists.

**[OpenWords](http://openwords.com/)**, an educational and social enterprise in the United States, builds language lessons and open-source tools for teachers and universities. Its founder, Marc Bogonovich, advises OFDN.

**[Council of Analytical Tribal Studies (COATS)](https://coatskoraput.org/)** is a research and academic institute in Koraput, Odisha, founded in 1993 to study the lives of Indigenous communities in the region. It runs MA, MPhil and PhD programmes and publishes research.

## Core values

- **People and community.** People are at the centre of any movement, so building community comes first for us.
- **Openness.** We release software under open standards, make our documentation open access and publish our datasets as open data.
- **Native languages.** The internet is English-centric, and that bias affects us too. We create resources in other languages to balance it.
- **STEM.** Science, technology, engineering and mathematics, with the open internet as the thread that ties them to our work.

In practice, this means research on languages, cultures and communities, mainly in India. It also means open educational resources, multimedia documentation of cultural heritage, and advocacy for the open web, digital security and privacy, done with government bodies, universities, community groups and individuals. We publish our work and methods openly.

## Diversity and inclusion

Most information on the internet is in English, and who creates and shares it follows patriarchy, caste and race. We research where communities and languages are missing online, and we direct our work to those gaps.
