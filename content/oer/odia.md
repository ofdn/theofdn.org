UNESCO considers Media and Information Literacy (MIL) as “*an important prerequisite for fostering equitable access to information and knowledge and promoting free, independent and pluralistic media and information systems*“. MIL is broadly based on the “*information and media in our everyday lives*“ and this page lists links to such resources in the Odia language.

The Odia language can be looked at both from a written standpoint (the *Standard Odia Written Language*) and from the oral perspective (*a [macrolanguage](http://www.sil.org/iso639-3/macrolanguages.asp) with over seven distinct languages and many dialects; the total number of speakers of all these sum to about 3,75,21,324 per [Indian Census 2011](https://www.censusindia.gov.in/2011Census/Language-2011/Statement-1.pdf)*). Language codes: “or” per [ISO 639-1](https://www.loc.gov/standards/iso639-2/php/langcodes_name.php?iso_639_1=or), “ori” per [ISO 639-2](https://www.loc.gov/standards/iso639-2/php/langcodes_name.php?code_ID=342), and “ori” per [ISO 639-3](https://iso639-3.sil.org/code/ori). (*see a general overview on [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Odia_language) and linguistic classification on [Glottolog](https://glottolog.org/resource/languoid/id/macr1269)*).

This article aims at providing a comprehensive list of Media and Information Literacy resources that might be useful for researchers, scholars and all other readers to learn about Odia.
