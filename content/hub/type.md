about: |
  Chapakala 19 revives a nineteenth‑century workhorse Odia letterpress typeface used in printed books, including work from the Orissa Mission Press in Cuttack (est. 1838). It focuses on the fonts used for body text rather than display styles, and follows the shapes and *juktakshara* (conjunct)s found in books printed between roughly 1810 and 1875. The font is built as a Unicode‑based OpenType family and is designed for reading, historical reprints and OCR training on scanned Odia books.

about: |
  Chapakala 20 extends Chapakala to a twentieth‑century workhorse Odia typeface that appeared across schoolbooks, magazines and general publishing till the end of the letterpress era (early 2000s). Together with Chapakala 19, it aims to cover the copy fonts used in almost all Odia letterpress‑printed books, so that editors, archivists and developers can work with two centuries of Odia print using a single, open type family. This typeface will also use Unicode encoding and OpenType features as Chapakala 19, with glyph shapes and conjuncts redrawn from twentieth‑century specimens.

<typeface>
name: Registry of Type Design
subtitle: Open records of typefaces, the people and the foundries who made them
colour: "#ffe8cc"
logo: /assets/logos/rtd.svg
about: |
  Registry of Type Design (RTD) is a free, human- and machine-readable registry of typefaces, the people who made them and the foundries, presses and institutions behind them. It covers digital and pre-digital type in every script. Each record is independently sourced, has a permanent identifier and is published as a citable page.

  RTD keeps bibliographic records for type design, much as library authority files do for authors and their books. As far as we know, it is the first open registry to do so. The data is open under CC BY-SA 4.0. Additionally, the records are machine-readable, supporting a JSON API, bulk dumps in CSV and NDJSON, and Dublin Core and MARCXML exports. Each ID also resolves as an ARK.

  Librarians and archivists can match the names in their catalogues against RTD with OpenRefine. Researchers can cite a single record or study the whole dataset. Type designers and foundries can check how their work is credited. Wikidata property P14791 links records both ways. In October 2026 RTD held 260 people, 180 typefaces and 34 foundries and other organisations, in 55 scripts.
