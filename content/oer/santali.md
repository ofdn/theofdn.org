UNESCO considers Media and Information Literacy (MIL) as “*an important prerequisite for fostering equitable access to information and knowledge and promoting free, independent and pluralistic media and information systems*“. MIL is broadly based on the “*information and media in our everyday lives*“ and this page lists links to such resources in the ****Santali** (ᱥᱟᱱᱛᱟᱲᱤ),** and its writing system ****Ol Chiki** (ᱚᱞ ᱪᱤᱠᱤ)**. Such resources are intended to be used by both Ho users and others alike. Generally, input tools, mobile apps, character encoding converters, Wikipedia and other Wikimedia projects, typefaces/fonts are the common resources included in such a directory.

### Background

[![Sarjom baha, a phonetic input method](/assets/images/santali-ol-chiki-sarjom-baha-input-method.webp)](https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/File:Sarjom_baha_input_method.svg)

<c>Keyboard layout of phonetic input method “Sarjom baha”. CC-BY-SA 4.0</c>

Santali is one of the indigenous languages  that is spoken by roughly about 7.6 million people (including [6.9 million native speakers in India](http://www.censusindia.gov.in/2011Census/Language-2011/Statement-1.pdf). Census 2011) that live mostly the eastern/northeastern states of India (Assam, Bihar, Chhattisgarh, Jharkhand, Mizoram, Odisha, Tripura and West Bengal). The Santali diaspora also lives outside India in Nepal, Bangladesh and Bhutan. The formal and the most widely writing system for Santali is Ol chiki/Ol cemet’ (*Unicode range: [1C50–1C7F;](https://www.unicode.org/charts/PDF/U1C50.pdf,) see [progress](https://scriptsource.org/cms/scripts/page.php?item_id=entry_detail&uid=rrkdvn7crf) of recent changes*) whereas Devanagari, Bengali and Odia have/are been used. Ol chiki is a non-abugida writing system that was developed by Pandit Raghunath Murmu in 1925. Santali is one of the very few Adivasi (indigenous) languages that is a part of the Eighth Schedule of the Indian Constitution ([*see provincial distribution*](http://Distribution%20of%20the%2022%20Scheduled%20Languages%20censusindia.gov.in/Census_Data_2001/Census_Data_Online/Language/parta.htm)). Despite of inclusion of the language in the elementary education system in a few Indian states (Jharkhand, Odisha and West Bengal), the written and online use of the language have a long way to go. Many native Santali speakers are constantly striving to put their language on the world map of late.

This page went live around the time when the Santali Wikipedia finally went live at <https://sat.wikipedia.org> in August 2018. Members of O Foundation in their volunteer contributor capacity as well as in professional roles were also involved in the development of input tools, typefaces, and organising outreach to grow the contributor community for creating and maintaining content, tools and other digital resources in Santali.

### Technical resources

#### Input tools

Input tools used to type in the Ol chiki writing system using a computer or a mobile device.

- ***Android***: Some of the popular ones include [GBoard by Google](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.google.android.inputmethod.latin), [Indic Keyboard Swalekh Flip](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.reverie.swalekh), [Multiling O Keyboard + emoji](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=kl.ime.oh)) (*also see:* [Santali notepad](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.braingen.santalinotepad), [1](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.santali.type.keyboard.free), [2](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.softmatic.zone.fancy.santali.keyboard), [3](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=pswm.santali.key), [4](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.keyboardlite.santali.free), [5](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.alicemccoy.keyboard.santali), [6](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.balintinfotech.santalikeyboardfree), [7](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=sky.santali.myphotokeyboard), [8](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.calvinfreeapps.santalikeyboard), [9](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.appsstyle.santali.keyboard), [10](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.fancy.fontforu.santalikeyboard))
- ***Desktop***: [Guru Gomke,](https://github.com/GuruGomke/files) cross-operating system input tool to type in Ol chiki that was developed during 2014-2015 by Jnanaranjan Sahu, Nasim Ali and Subhashish Panigrahi ([project page](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Project_Ol_chiki)) at the Centre for Internet Society’s Access to Knowledge program
